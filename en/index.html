01 /Approach

The scans are already there.The reserve is not yet read.

Before chemotherapy or stem cell transplantation for a haematological cancer, almost every patient has a CT or PET/CT and most have a brain MRI. These scans show muscle, fat, spine and white matter, yet after staging they are rarely read again.

Chronological age separates resilience only roughly. Geriatric assessment captures the difference but takes time. 4A4P — Geriatric Anatomy and Performance, the GAP — tests whether this gap can be closed without an extra scan or extra radiation.

03 /Question

Three models.One question.

Population: aged 60 or older, haematological cancer, before chemotherapy or stem cell transplantation.

Outcome: death within 90 days of the first treatment dose, or a new need for help in at least one basic activity of daily living.

In research · no results yet
  1. 01

    Clinical model

    M1

    Age, sex, ECOG, comorbidity burden and a disease-specific index.

  2. 02

    + Performance

    M2

    The four bedside tests are added. Do they add value?

  3. 03

    + Anatomy

    M3

    The four imaging domains are added. The main question: does imaging add anything?

One model is fitted; diagnosis group and treatment type enter as covariates, and calibration is reported separately in each subgroup.

04 /Principles

Rules first.Results after.

  • Archive first.

    After ethics approval the work starts with retrospective records; the model is then tested again in a prospective cohort.

  • Rules in advance.

    Measurement definitions and the analysis plan are registered, with dates, before the data are examined.

  • Images stay in-house.

    Segmentation runs on a local computer; images are not sent to the cloud and no identities are stored.

  • No invented numbers.

    A value that cannot be measured stays empty with its reason. No coefficient or risk score is published before validation.
